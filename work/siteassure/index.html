Transcript

This is a safety log for construction crews. It works offline, it's driven by voice, and nothing in it can be quietly changed.

A field note starts at the mic. This build transcribes it on the laptop's own graphics card, with no network, so the note never leaves the device. Here's one from a highway job. I'll start recording. Crew was on the shoulder next to live traffic, setting up a lane closure. Flagger was in position. We had an open trench along the median, with no shoring in yet. When I stop, it comes back as text in about three seconds, without leaving this laptop.

Every word came through. The raw transcript is kept untouched, beside the cleaned-up version, and any summary is shown alongside it, never in place of it. I set the trade to highway and bridge, and confirm the details.

Two flags: work-zone traffic control, and an open trench. For highway work, forty-one percent of inspections are cited, at about twenty-one thousand seven hundred dollars each. I accept both, and save.

The record is saved, and its audit verifies. Resolving the trench flag takes a note, appended to the chain as a new version. And the note can be spoken, too.

Shoring box dropped in at two p.m. The competent person signed off.

A couple of seconds later, it's in the note field, and I resolve with proof. The fix lands as version two, above the original record, which stays exactly as it was. Nothing is overwritten.

Every change is chained and hash-verified: the record, its audio, and the fix, three entries under one head hash. The same records feed a trade-risk dashboard, benchmarked against two years of OSHA enforcement in the New York area, with open flags counted by site.

Each entry's hash includes the one before it, so nothing can be silently altered or removed. HouseCheck's export uses this same chain, two cycles later. It was proven here first.

01What it is

A construction site's safety record is a legal document before it is anything else. SiteAssure keeps it append-only: every create, amend and delete is chained and hash-verified, so nothing is silently altered or removed.

02How it works

Each entry's hash includes the one before it.

  • Editing any historical record breaks every hash after it, so tampering is detectable by anyone holding the file, not only whoever runs the server.
  • Network egress is cut to an explicit allowlist rather than a kill-switch, so a new call site cannot quietly reach the internet by default.
  • The same records, read a second way, drive a trade-risk dashboard with no extra data entry.

03What it doesn't do

Feature-complete and uninstallable.

  • There is no signed installer yet, so running it means building from source. That is tracked as the next task rather than hidden.
  • This is the hash chain HouseCheck's export uses two cycles later. It was proven here first, on OSHA records.

Recorded from v0.1.4, built from source with CUDA, on an empty database. The field note and the resolution note are spoken into the app's mic input through a virtual audio cable, and the app transcribes both on the GPU. Narration and notes are a synthetic clone of my own voice, made on my machine; made with Claude Code.
